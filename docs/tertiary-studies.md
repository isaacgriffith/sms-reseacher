# Tertiary Studies

> **This file was empty until 2026-08-07.** Its content now lives in
> [`docs/methodology/04-tertiary.md`](./methodology/04-tertiary.md), written from Kitchenham et al.
> 2010, 2009 and 2013 — the SE tertiary studies that established the method.

A tertiary study is a secondary study whose primary studies are themselves secondary studies. There
is **no separate tertiary methodology**: the SLR process is applied with secondary studies as the
unit of analysis, so [`01-slr.md`](./methodology/01-slr.md) applies throughout.

What is specific to a tertiary study:

- **Terminology dominates the search.** You are searching for a *method*, and authors describe it
  inconsistently — "literature survey", "assembly of studies", or just "review". Use many simple
  search strings rather than one complex string.
- **DARE is the quality instrument**, because the primary studies are secondary studies.
- **A defined multi-rater extraction protocol exists** — "consensus and minority report" — in which
  the third reviewer supplies an independent reading but is deliberately excluded from adjudicating.
- **Mapping studies score lower on DARE for structural reasons**, so scores are not comparable across
  review types without saying so.

See [`docs/methodology/04-tertiary.md`](./methodology/04-tertiary.md) for the full process, the
scoring anchors, the worked attrition figures, and the caveats.
